## How it should sound

### The voice

- First person on the site: "I built", not "Charles built". The résumé page may
  use short fragments that start with a past-tense verb.
- Plain verbs: built, fixed, wrote, shipped, moved, found, cut, set up.
- Specific nouns beat adjectives. "M-Pesa STK Push", "KRA eTIMS", "row-level
  security" and "a balanced journal entry" are what make it believable.
  Specific detail is the strongest sign a person wrote it.
- Short sentences, with the odd longer one. Paragraphs of different lengths.
- Contractions are fine: I'm, don't, it's.
- Say what he did, then why it mattered, then stop. No summary line at the end.
- Modest and exact. "I'm learning C# by building Salio" beats "proficient in
  .NET".
- American spelling, as written in Kenya: color, organize, center.
- Every claim survives a follow-up question in an interview. If Charles couldn't
  talk about it for two minutes, cut it.  use inspos of words fromat and pattern from well known portfolios

### What gives AI copy away: remove all of it

**Words.** Cut them or use a plain word instead:
leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, elevate, empower,
harness, unlock, unleash, delve, journey, passionate, innovative, dynamic,
transformative, world-class, best-in-class, bespoke, pixel-perfect,
game-changer, holistic, streamline, ecosystem, landscape, realm, tapestry,
synergy, foster, testament, pivotal, paramount, meticulous, elegant,
"navigate" used figuratively, "craft" as a verb for software, "solutions" for
what he makes, "digital experiences", "drive results", "deliver value", "bring
ideas to life", "at the intersection of", "in today's fast-paced world", "take
it to the next level", "I thrive", "I'm dedicated to", "obsessed with".

**Patterns.**
- Lists of three everywhere ("fast, secure and scalable"). Use the number of
  items that's true. Two is fine. One is often better.
- "Not just X, it's Y" and "X isn't just about Y".
- "Whether you're X or Y…" and "From X to Y…" openers.
- Rhetorical questions.
- Em dashes. None in English copy: use a full stop, a comma or brackets.
- Headings with a colon and a subtitle ("Quality: the foundation of
  everything").
- Every bullet starting with an -ing word, or every bullet the same length.
- Closing one-liners ("Because great software is built, not born.").
- Sentences true of any developer. Test: swap in another name. If the sentence
  still works, it says nothing.
- Mission talk ("I'm on a mission to…").
- "Let's build something amazing together", exclamation marks, emoji.

### Hiring: what a recruiter needs, in order

A recruiter gives the home page about 30 seconds. In that time they must learn:

1. Who he is and what he does: role, what he builds, who for, where.
2. Proof: two or three real projects, each with what it does and one hard thing
   he solved.
3. That he's available, and how to reach him.

Rules that follow from that:

- The hero says what he builds and for whom, not what he values.
- Project text leads with what the thing does, then his part ("I built it on my
  own", "for a client"), then the hard problem, then the stack.
- In-progress work: don't apologise for it and don't dress it up. "Salio is in
  stage 2 of 6" is fine.
- Contact text is one plain sentence plus the email.
- Philosophy and capabilities: tie each principle to something he actually did.
  If there's no real example for a principle, write the shortest honest line and
  list it under "Outside scope" as a candidate to cut.
